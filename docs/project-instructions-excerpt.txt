Objectives
In this project, you will work in teams of 2, 3, or 4 to conduct a technical audit of an automated
decision system (ADS) of your choice with clear scope based on team size. We suggest that
you audit one of the systems developed in response to a Kaggle competition of your choice, but
you should feel free to use other systems that are of interest to you. Do not focus on
Northpointe’s COMPAS in this assignment, since this tool was already covered extensively
during class. Be sure to prominently cite your sources of code and data!
All team members should work together on all parts of the project. You should not discuss
your project submission or components of your solution with any students other than your
project partner. If you have questions about this assignment, please send a private question to
all instructors on Piazza.
Detailed description and goals
In this project, we encourage you to focus on examples from Kaggle competitions, where the
goals, the data, and one or several implementations are available for analysis. Select a Kaggle
competition that has already finished, and for which you can find and successfully execute at
least one solution. A list of solutions to Kaggle competitions is available here, and you may be
able to find solutions in other ways. If you decide to work with a system that’s not from Kaggle,
you should make sure that data and at least one implementation is available to you. Once
again: Be sure to prominently cite your sources of code and data!

Note on building on existing analysis
Some of the analysis this project asks for may already exist: the Kaggle competition page
typically describes the data and the task, and the solution notebook you are auditing may
include data profiling, validation results, or even some subgroup analysis. You may use and
build on this material, but two rules apply.
● First, cite it prominently and precisely: say in the report, at the point where you use it,
whose analysis it is, give a link that lets us access it, and if you reproduce a figure or
table, label it as reproduced.
● Second, your report must contain substantial analysis of your own.
Restating existing results, even with full citation, does not satisfy the requirements of Sections 2,
4, and 5. Where you build on others’ work, make clear what they did and what you added.
Unattributed reuse of code, figures, or text is an academic integrity violation; attributed reuse
without your own contribution will be graded as if the corresponding section were missing.
